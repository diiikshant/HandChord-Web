- Finger-classification approach: long fingers use MCP/PIP/DIP/tip bend angles, palm-centre distance, and local palm-axis direction. The thumb uses CMC/MCP/IP/tip geometry, palm and index-MCP distance, and outward separation on the local index-to-little axis. Low-visibility required landmarks return `unclear`.

- Open-palm thumb repair: a diagonal, naturally bent open thumb could satisfy one strict folded threshold and make an open palm report four. Thumb classification now requires several agreeing local-geometry signals before it marks a thumb folded, while an extended thumb needs several signals of separation, reach, and joint extension.
- Fist thumb repair: physical Chrome testing showed a closed fist with all four long fingers correctly folded but its thumb left `unclear`. A compact thumb resting across curled fingers is now recognised as folded when its local palm reach, index-MCP distance, and non-outward direction agree; this remains separate from the open-palm rule.

- Web Audio architecture: `src/components/AudioTest.tsx` is the button-only interface. `src/music/MusicTheoryEngine.ts` generates scales and diatonic triads; `src/audio/AudioEngine.ts` owns one lazily created AudioContext, active-chord state, instrument selection, output safety, and cleanup; `src/audio/PolySynth.ts` creates preset-configured oscillator voices and envelopes. Deterministic tests live beside the music and audio modules.
- Audio startup behaviour: Audio is disabled until **Enable Audio** is selected. That user action creates or resumes the single AudioContext. The UI shows disabled, starting, ready, suspended, and error states, including **Resume Audio** if the browser suspends it.
- Audio Test controls: all 12 chromatic root keys, Major and Natural Minor scales, master volume, six diatonic chord buttons, Stop, and an A4 440 Hz test tone. Chords are generated algorithmically, not listed separately for each key.
- Instrument architecture: reusable definitions in `src/audio/instruments/` hold each ID, description, oscillator layers, ADSR envelope, low-pass filter, octave offset, gain compensation, and optional auto-release. `PolySynth` reads the active definition only when it creates a new voice, keeping the one AudioContext and existing effects intact.
- Instruments added: **Warm Pad** (default; triangle/saw blend, slow 180 ms attack, 900 ms release), **Soft Keys** (sine/triangle, fast 25 ms attack), **Pluck** (triangle/saw, 8 ms attack, 450 ms automatic release), **Organ** (sine/triangle/saw harmonics, near-full sustain), and **Deep Bass** (sine/triangle, one octave lower, 900 Hz low-pass).
- Instrument gain safety: preset per-voice compensation ranges from 8% to 13%; Deep Bass uses the lowest value to avoid excessive low-frequency level. All voices still pass through the fixed performance gain, manual master gain, and existing compressor.
- Instrument switching and persistence: selecting a different preset releases the current chord cleanly, does not create a second AudioContext or effect graph, and does not change key, scale, calibration, tracking, or effect values. The selected ID is stored in localStorage and restored after refresh; invalid or unavailable stored data falls back to Warm Pad.
- Personal-sound architecture: `SoundSource` distinguishes built-in presets from IndexedDB-backed personal samples, while `AudioEngine` routes both to the same shared effects input. `SampleInstrument` owns only one-use `AudioBufferSourceNode` voices; it creates a new source and gain envelope for every instrument-mode note or one-shot trigger, releases old voices on chord changes/Stop/source changes, and cleans them up after release.
- Microphone-recording architecture: `MicrophoneRecorder` owns an audio-only `getUserMedia({ audio: true, video: false })` stream, MediaRecorder session, countdown, maximum-duration timer, and microphone-track cleanup. `RecordingPanel` presents the recording flow, asks AudioEngine to create/resume its existing AudioContext for decoding and meter analysis, then hands the local blob to the existing sample editor. It never owns camera tracks, and camera cleanup never owns microphone tracks.
- Recording flow and states: Record Sound opens an explanation without requesting permission. **Start Recording** feature-detects a MediaRecorder MIME type, requests microphone permission, runs a 3/2/1 countdown, records for up to 10 seconds, and shows Record Again / Use Recording / Cancel after local processing. States cover not requested, requesting permission, granted, denied, unavailable, busy, countdown, recording, stopped, processing, and error with Try Again for recovery.
- Recording format and input meter: MIME selection checks `audio/webm;codecs=opus`, then `audio/webm`, then `audio/ogg;codecs=opus` with `MediaRecorder.isTypeSupported`; no format is assumed blindly. An AudioEngine-owned `MediaStreamAudioSourceNode → AnalyserNode` meter updates at about 12.5 times per second and is never routed to speakers or used as the recorder input.
- Recording silence suggestion: leading/trailing samples below a conservative 0.015 amplitude threshold are suggested as editor trim boundaries; quiet content in the middle is untouched. Nearly silent recordings keep the full duration, warn the user, and default normalisation off to avoid amplifying noise. The original recording bytes are preserved until the user saves.
- Instrument sample looping: **Loop Sample** is optional and defaults to off. When enabled for Play as Instrument, every pitched voice sets `source.loop = true` and repeats only its trimmed region until its chord releases. One-shot sounds always ignore looping and play once at original pitch.
- Loop region and boundary handling: the trimmed start/end are the loop start/end; separate editable loop points remain out of scope. A loop needs at least 120 ms of audio. A default 30 ms edge fade is clamped to the smaller of 50 ms or one quarter of the loop duration, then applied to a local processed playback buffer at the loop edges. It reduces boundary clicks but may create a subtle level dip; advanced seamless crossfade editing is intentionally deferred.
- Loop voice cleanup: on chord change, Stop, gesture-audio disable, preset/source change, page visibility loss, or disposal, each looping voice ramps its gain down, calls `stop()` after its release, disconnects on `ended`, and is removed from the active voice map. Older `ended` callbacks cannot remove newer voices because every voice has a unique ID.
- Supported personal audio: browser-decodable WAV, MP3, M4A/AAC, and OGG are accepted through the file picker. The safe limits are 12 MB and 10 seconds. Files are decoded with the existing enabled AudioContext only and are never uploaded.
- Sample editor and trim behaviour: waveform peaks are downsampled locally from mono or stereo buffers. The editor stores trim metadata with the original audio bytes, so a saved sound plays only its selected region without repeatedly copying audio data. Trimmed regions must be at least 80 ms. Default fade-in and fade-out are 15 ms.
- Sample processing: normalisation targets a peak of 0.85 with a maximum 4× gain, while near-silent files remain at unity gain. Reverse is applied locally to the decoded buffer at playback. Instrument-mode samples can loop safely inside the trimmed region; one-shots are unlooped and preserve original pitch.
- Root note and transposition: Instrument mode uses a manually selected root note (C2–B5) and `2^(semitones/12)` playback-rate calculation. Rates are clamped to 0.5×–2×; chord notes beyond one octave select a closer octave first to avoid extreme chipmunk or muddy playback. One-shot mode keeps playback rate at 1×.
- IndexedDB persistence: database `handchord-personal-sounds`, version 3, stores validated metadata plus original `ArrayBuffer` audio data in the `sounds` store. Loop-enabled, loop start, loop end, edge-fade duration, source type, original MIME type, and recording duration are persisted. Version 3 safely normalises earlier records that lack recording metadata or have only the original `loop` boolean. Invalid/missing records are ignored safely; deleting a sound removes its stored bytes and falls back to Warm Pad if it was active. localStorage is used only for small built-in-preference data.
- Files added: `src/audio/sounds/soundTypes.ts`, `sampleMath.ts`, `SampleInstrument.ts`, `personalSoundStorage.ts`, and `sampleMath.test.ts`; `src/components/SampleWaveform.tsx`, `SampleEditor.tsx`, `PersonalSounds.tsx`, and `RecordingPanel.tsx` implement the lightweight local editor/library. `src/recording/recordingMath.ts`, `MicrophoneRecorder.ts`, `recordingMath.test.ts`, and `src/hooks/useMicrophoneRecording.ts` provide the isolated recording flow.
- Sampled chord-duration synchronisation: pitch shifting correctly changes `playbackRate`, but it also changes the natural wall-clock duration of a trimmed buffer. `SampleInstrument` now creates an explicit sampled voice group per Instrument-mode chord, with one 15 ms lookahead start timestamp, shared release time, shared final-silence time, and group-level cleanup.
- Non-looping group duration: each member calculates `playable trimmed duration ÷ final clamped playback rate`; the group uses the shortest resulting natural duration. This intentionally truncates lower notes so all chord members become silent together. Reversed samples use the same selected-region duration.
- Looping group behaviour: looping members still repeat their individual trimmed regions while held, but a chord change, Stop, source change, Gesture Audio disable, or page hide gives every member the same release and stop schedule.
- Envelope safety: attack is capped at 35% of the shared duration and release at the smaller of the requested fade, 45% of the shared duration, and the duration remaining after attack. A region that becomes shorter than 12 ms after final rate clamping fails safely rather than scheduling invalid automation.
- Debug diagnostics: Personal Sounds now shows natural member durations, group count, member count, shared duration, and the shared start/release/stop schedule for development testing.

- Composition-loop recording: one session-only 4/4 loop can now be captured from HandChord’s internal performance route. The composition recorder receives post-effect audio after the fixed 100% performance gain, before the manual master gain and final compressor. It never records a microphone, metronome, manual output level, final compressor, or existing loop playback.
- Transport: tempo is 40–220 BPM (default 100); loop lengths are 1, 2, 4, or 8 bars (default 4); count-in is Off, 1, or 2 bars (default 1). Musical timing uses AudioContext time and the exact 4/4 equations, while requestAnimationFrame updates only the UI readout.
- Exact internal PCM capture: `CompositionPcmRecorder` registers one AudioWorklet per AudioContext and arms it with `startFrame`, `endFrame`, and `expectedFrameCount`. The worklet captures only that frame interval in bounded chunks. Extra frames are trimmed and any recoverable shortage is silence-padded before creating the exact-length AudioBuffer.
- Metronome: native oscillator clicks are routed directly to the manual master route, not the performance recording tap. Beat one is emphasised. It can be enabled during count-in and recording, but is excluded from the saved loop.
- Playback and feedback safety: recorded loop playback is a separate one-use AudioBufferSourceNode routed to manual master gain and compressor only. It never feeds the recording tap, so live performance can continue over the loop without accidental overdub or recursion. Both-fist emergency Stop releases live voices and also stops loop playback; it never deletes the loop.
- Boundary and tails: a 10 ms equal-power wraparound blend is applied only at the end boundary and clamped to 20 ms or one eighth of loop length. The buffer stays its exact musical duration. Reverb and tape-delay tails outside the selected bars are intentionally not captured, so very wet/feedback-heavy settings may still reveal a loop boundary.
- Replacement and undo: Re-record stops playback and retains the old loop until a new exact PCM capture succeeds. Clear and successful replacement each preserve one in-memory undo loop with its buffer and metadata. Loops are session-only and are removed on refresh; Personal Sounds remain unchanged in IndexedDB.

- Final gesture mapping: anatomical left stable one/two/three/four/open-palm selects positions 1–5. Anatomical right open palm selects the primary bank; right index-only selects the secondary bank. Major primary maps I, ii, iii, IV, V. Major secondary maps vi, vii°, ♭VII, iv, and V/vi. Natural Minor uses algorithmically generated i, ii°, III, iv, v primary chords and musically valid VI, VII, ♭VII, iv, and V/VI secondary equivalents.
- Gesture-audio responsiveness repair: physical Chrome testing found that stable finger recognition (already 200–300 ms) plus a second 300 ms chord confirmation felt delayed. The controller confirmation is now 100 ms, so it still uses only stable gestures but does not double the wait. The controller also reads MediaPipe's live hand count directly, preventing retained diagnostic records from delaying a missing-hand release.
- Gesture timing: a complete left/right mapping must remain unchanged for 100 ms after each hand has become stable. A last stable gesture for each anatomical hand survives 650 ms of uncertainty. A current chord survives unclear tracking for up to 900 ms. Both stable fists must remain for 500 ms to stop; both hands missing for 850 ms releases the gesture-owned chord, followed by the synth's 200 ms smooth release.
- Hand-role behaviour: the controller uses anatomical MediaPipe roles rather than screen position. It retains a recent left/right role during short unresolved or missing frames and does not change roles when the hands cross.
- Four-axis movement model: anatomical left vertical controls reverb, left horizontal controls distortion, right vertical controls tape delay, and right horizontal controls chorus width. The existing palm tracker converts coordinates once into user-visible mirrored space, so visual-left maps to a lower horizontal value and visual-right maps to a higher value for both hands. The chorus mapper reverses only its own right-horizontal input so inward/leftward movement increases chorus.
- Palm-centre method: `src/movement/PalmPositionTracker.ts` averages wrist, index MCP, middle MCP, ring MCP, and little MCP. It converts MediaPipe coordinates once into the mirrored, user-visible coordinate space, where visual-left/lower are 0 and visual-right/upper are 1.
- Calibration architecture: one `CalibrationService` owns the shared profile of four independent ranges (`leftVertical`, `leftHorizontal`, `rightVertical`, `rightHorizontal`). Each explicit update replaces only its own range. `CalibrationStorage` validates and stores the profile in localStorage. `CalibrationView` captures a median over about 700 ms and rejects missing, low-confidence, or too-narrow ranges.
- Movement smoothing: normalised values clamp to 0–1, use a 5% output dead zone, and use exponential smoothing with a 0.22 update amount. A missing hand retains its last safe values for 500 ms; afterward it remains safely unchanged and is marked temporarily unavailable while the other hand continues.
- Reverb implementation: `src/audio/ReverbEffect.ts` builds one reusable dry/wet ConvolverNode graph in the existing AudioContext. `PolySynth` feeds the reverb input; dry and wet paths join the existing master gain, compressor, and destination. No graph is rebuilt for a movement update.
- Impulse response and mapping: the app generates a local two-channel decaying-noise impulse response at audio startup, with a 6-second decay and slightly different left/right decay curves. `leftVertical` uses a squared response from 0% to 95% wet; the midpoint is about 24% wet. Gain targets use a 100 ms time constant, settling over roughly 300 ms.
- Reverb safety and tracking loss: Reverb Control is 10% wet while off or when leftVertical is missing. It will not activate from movement until leftVertical is calibrated. A lost left hand keeps its existing movement value for 500 ms and afterward keeps its last safe reverb setting while reporting unavailable.
- Gesture-controlled volume removed: right vertical no longer changes volume. `AudioEngine` retains a fixed performance-gain stage at 100%, independent of all hand movement. The manual Audio Test master slider remains the overall safety control and now defaults to 100%; this is safe for the initial synth because the existing compressor remains the final output limiter and the effect paths use conservative gain compensation.
- Tape-delay implementation: `src/audio/TapeDelayEffect.ts` builds one reusable dry path plus `DelayNode → low-pass filter → feedback gain` loop before reverb. The feedback is capped below 1.0, filtered repeats are darker than the dry chord, and no delay nodes are rebuilt while the hand moves.
- Tape-delay parameters: rightVertical maps upward through a weighted squared curve to 100–700 ms delay time, 0–65% wet mix, and 0–55% feedback. At the midpoint the delay is about 280 ms, 20% wet, and 21% feedback. The repeat filter cutoff is fixed at 3.5 kHz.
- Tape-delay smoothing and tracking loss: wet/dry changes use a 70 ms target time constant (about 210 ms to settle), feedback uses 100 ms (about 300 ms), and delay time uses 120 ms (about 360 ms). A missing right hand retains its last safe right-vertical value for 500 ms; afterward it keeps that delay setting while reporting unavailable. Turning Tape Delay Control off safely returns wet and feedback to 0%.
- Calibration mapping: `rightVertical` is now labelled Tape Delay in the calibration summary, guided lower/upper steps, and its individual recalibration action. The valid saved range remains intact and does not overwrite the other three axis ranges.

Missing calibration or a disabled control uses a clean 0% wet signal.
